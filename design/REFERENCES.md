# Reference research for round 2

Round 1 failed because its directions were invented aesthetics. Round 2 is
designed from references: a survey of major editorial pages and a survey of
prominent peers' personal sites, both run 2026-07-09. This file records the
findings the new directions must answer to.

## Peers: prominent writer-academics' personal sites

Twelve live sites analysed (Sara Ahmed, feministkilljoys, Jack Halberstam,
Kit Heyam, Travis Alabanza, CN Lester, Carmen Maria Machado, Rebecca Solnit,
Emma Holten, Katherine Angel, Amia Srinivasan, Roxane Gay, Leonora Christina
Skov), plus instructive absences (Maggie Nelson, Shon Faye, and every Danish
kønsforsker: none has a real personal site; the Danish tier lives on
university research portals, forlag pages, and speaker bureaus).

What the genre does: name as masthead, five-to-seven item nav (About, Books,
Writing, Events, Press, Contact), one page per book, a single credited
portrait, newsletter capture, platform-default typography (Squarespace or
WordPress, interchangeable sans-serifs, white ground). The genre is
content-credible and design-anonymous; nobody's typography could be
identified blind.

What separates credible from neglected: currency (Halberstam's site has been
dead since 2014 and damages him; sites architected around a blog feed die,
sites architected around the current book stay true for years), template
hygiene ("Your Custom Text Here" is live on Sara Ahmed's homepage), leading
with a claim rather than a name (Solnit's hope quote, Holten's "value of
care"), and explicit doors per audience (Ahmed's stated fees, Heyam's
per-service pages, Alabanza's Press tab).

Books done well (the Machado–Holten formula): large cover; one devastating
named pull-quote rather than a blurb wall; compact recognition list; a
two-sentence framing line for a new book; buy routes ordered publisher and
independent first; translations shown as a stature grid.

Open goals nobody in the genre takes: a real press kit (photos with stated
credits, short/long bio, name and pronoun guidance); routing readers by
register (only Srinivasan splits Research from Criticism); giving anything
away free (Mio's free book has no precedent in the cohort and is her
differentiator); positively-framed engagement terms; design that carries
meaning at all; semantic HTML and structured data.

Implications fixed for round 2, whatever the visual direction:

1. Lead with the claim and the free book, not the CV or a feed. The hero
   action is "Hent bogen gratis" or equivalent.
2. Three doors above the fold or near it: practitioners and institutions
   (foredrag, undervisning), press (a real press kit), readers (bog, essays,
   digte).
3. Writing structured by register: Bøger, Fagfællebedømt forskning,
   Formidling og essays, Digte. Each with full, honest citations.
4. Ikke Bare Køn is a named platform inside miobirk.com, not an orphan
   second brand.
5. Danish first; an English layer for research peers can come later.
6. The comparison bar in Denmark is emmaholten.com; the field is otherwise
   empty. A typographically deliberate site outclasses the entire genre
   without raising its voice.
